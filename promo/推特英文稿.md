## 口播英文对照（做英文字幕用）

> 和中文口播一段一段对应。录完的中文口播要是改了说法，这里跟着改。

**缘起**　Hi everyone. This time I made a fan project based on Mononoke. I love this series, especially its patterns and colors. Tripo was running a co-creation program that came with a lot of free credits, so I wanted to see if I could build the Medicine Seller's world with World Labs, Blender and Tripo.

**开头**　It turned into a small puzzle game you can play from start to finish. Today I'll play through it with you.

**推门**　The story is set in the Ōoku from the movie The Phantom in the Rain: the women's quarters of the shogun's castle in the Edo period. Push the door open and you're in this corridor. The scenes come from World Labs. I draw a panorama and give it to World Labs, and it generates a Gaussian splat space you can walk into. Splats have no collision, so I worked out the walkable area from the point cloud and used it as the boundary. The hardest part was putting things inside. Characters and props are separate models. At first they floated in the air, and their outlines showed through the walls.

**规则**　The gameplay is inspired by Rusty Lake, which I love: the spaces are small, and every object holds part of a story. The rule comes from the original series: the Medicine Seller has to learn the mononoke's Form, Truth and Reason before he can draw his sword. I turned that into a scale. Put two matching items on it, and when it balances, one word lights up.

**形**　To the left of the entrance there's a painting of a girl kicking a temari, and on the floor there's a piece of colored thread. Match them, and Form lights up. The scale also works as a compass. The closer you get to a clue, the harder it shakes, and it rings a bell. If you stand still for a while, it gives you a hint. I changed the controls once. At first you walked freely with WASD, but when I played it myself the camera kept spinning around, so I switched to click-to-move.

**真**　The first room on the right belongs to the maids. Kame gives you half of a letter from home. Then go across the hall and look through the palace entry register on Utayama's desk, and Truth lights up. Put the two together and the story becomes clear: when a maid named Yuki entered the Ōoku, she brought a temari her mother had wound for her, and it was taken from her at the gate. With the characters, what I struggled with most was how faithful to be. I wanted people to recognize them at a glance, but I didn't want to trace the originals. So I redid the design sheets in my own palette, posed them in an A-pose and gave them to Tripo. The flat colors, outlines and washi texture were added afterwards in code.

**理**　Last is Reason. You can fish that temari out of the well. Temari were common in the Edo period, often wound by mothers for their daughters from leftover kimono silk. In Yuki's empty room at the end of the hall there's also a wooden spool and an unfinished letter home. You can only put one of them on the scale, and your choice decides which ending you see.

**拔剑**　With all three words lit, the Medicine Seller draws his sword. None of these moves are stock animations; I posed them one by one. The horizontal slash caused a problem: his legs stretched his big sleeves into a sheet. So I split his robes and hair into layers and hung a chain of bones in each, so they swing on their own. His feet had trouble too. On the slash they folded right up, like he'd learned bone origami.

**分工**　Claude Code wrote most of the code. I ran several sessions: one as the lead, and the others in charge of characters, scenes, gameplay and writing. Each one only edited its own files, and they all shared one to-do list. My job was to make the calls: what to do first, whether it looked right, and whether something was worth the credits. Having used it, Opus is genuinely great.

**感想**　Back to the movie. The Phantom in the Rain looks very different from the TV series: the screen is full of patterns and color, almost maximalist, and I love it. When my mom saw this, she said I should make a Tang or Song dynasty character next. So next time, I want to pick a few interesting characters from Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio.

**结尾**　Right now I'm building the inn from the TV series. This time the scenes are made from modular pieces in Blender. Splats look great, but they blur when you get close to a wall, and you can't wander freely. Modular pieces are less detailed, but there's a lot more room to walk. I'll open-source it when it's done; the code is on GitHub. Finally, thanks to the Medicine Seller for the inspiration. I hope you enjoy it.
